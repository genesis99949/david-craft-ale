# David Craft Ale — QA report

Date: 2026-09-04  
Scope: standard static audit of the nine root HTML pages, shared scripts, styles, local links and referenced assets.

## Result

No release-blocking structural errors were found. The website is technically coherent enough for a final visual pass and Behance capture preparation.

## Verified

- Nine root pages parse with one H1 each and `lang="en"`.
- No duplicate IDs, broken internal links, missing local assets or missing image `alt` attributes were found.
- All JavaScript files pass syntax checks and CSS brace counts balance.
- External links opened in a new tab use `noopener`.
- Beer routes, gallery navigation, cart data and the related-products carousel pass the existing regression scripts.
- All sixteen beer SKUs use 330 ml and the approved prices: bottle 2.49 USD, can 1.99 USD, bottle six-pack 13.99 USD, can six-pack 10.99 USD.
- Index and Products now provide a keyboard skip link. The Products account icon now opens `account.html`.
- The broken Blonde Ale dash and the honey-related privacy phrase were corrected.

## Remaining before Behance publication

### Medium priority

4. **Optimize catalog images** — 93 referenced assets total about 50.3 MB. The largest page assets are 1.3–2.6 MB PNG product renders, particularly cans, six-packs and accessories. Add responsive WebP versions before hosting or recording page loads.
5. **Clean the asset folder** — `assets` contains about 190.8 MB, leaving roughly 140 MB in unused masters, previous generations and intermediate variants. Keep an archive outside the published build and deploy only consumed assets.
6. **Add sharing metadata after the public URL is known** — all pages currently lack canonical URLs, Open Graph images and Twitter card metadata. Several titles and descriptions are also shorter than typical search-preview targets. These require the final domain and a selected social cover.

### Launch-only checks

8. Run Lighthouse, validate HTTPS/security headers, caching, 404 behavior, schema, sitemap and `robots.txt` on the deployed URL.

## Current limitation

## Visual audit — Homepage

Inputs: full-page captures at 375, 768 and 1440 CSS px, supplied at 2× pixel density.

### Overall assessment

- The desktop composition has a strong, consistent art direction and clear section rhythm. Hero, beer selector, Hopper feature, story panel and closing sections feel like one brand system.
- The mobile hierarchy survives well: the headline fits, bottles remain recognizable, the beer selector communicates horizontal movement, CTAs stay comfortably tappable and no horizontal page overflow is visible.
- The 768 px layout is the weakest breakpoint. Two desktop-positioned patterns do not adapt cleanly to the available width.

### Confirmed findings

1. **Medium — Hero responsibility icons overlap the Dark bottle at 768 px.** The icons remain absolutely positioned until 720 px, so the first two marks enter the product silhouette at the 768 px breakpoint. Move the inline/mobile treatment to a wider breakpoint, around 900 px, or reserve a clear column for them.
2. **Medium — Reviews form a 3 + 1 grid at 768 px.** The fourth card sits alone on the second row and leaves more than half the section empty. Force a balanced 2 × 2 grid between mobile and desktop widths.
3. **Low — Mobile reviews are visually long.** Four large cards follow an already long story section, producing the densest part of the page. Reduce card minimum height and vertical padding on narrow screens, or use a horizontal snap carousel if motion is desirable.
4. **Low — Mobile story copy is harder to scan than the rest of the page.** The long centered paragraph creates many similarly weighted lines. Split it into two short paragraphs and consider left alignment below 480 px.

### What passed visually

- No clipped hero headline, bottles, product cards or primary CTAs were found at the three supplied widths.
- Touch targets, card radii, borders, shadows and spacing remain internally consistent.
- The 375 px beer carousel clearly exposes the next card and includes arrows, instruction text and position dots.
- Hopper scales successfully from side-by-side desktop presentation to a stacked mobile feature without losing its labels or CTA hierarchy.
- The story artwork keeps a clean crop at all three widths.
- Text contrast is strong in the hero, product, review and affiliate sections.

### Capture artifacts and remaining interaction checks

- The pale, partially filled “Made for Good Company” headline is controlled by a scroll-linked ink-fill animation. A full-page screenshot freezes the page at the top and therefore does not represent the headline while the user reaches it.
- The sticky footer content is absent from all three stitched full-page captures. The peach area is visible, but the footer itself must be checked with a normal viewport screenshot taken after manually scrolling to the bottom.
- Static captures cannot verify the loader, menu opening, carousel drag, beer selection, reveal timing, smooth scrolling, video playback, hover states or cart behavior.

### Tablet follow-up applied

- The responsibility icons now leave the hero artwork and sit in a centered row below the bottles through 900 px.
- The 521–850 px beer detail card keeps its copy on the left and turns the CTA into a full-height right panel with a curved inner edge; the compact mobile card remains stacked.
- The Hopper render, halo and ring are smaller between 641 and 959 px, and its headline uses balanced wrapping.
- The Story heading now uses the standard reveal instead of scroll-linked color filling.
- At tablet widths the 16:9 Story media uses a wider 16:10 frame with a lighter crop; the portrait mobile treatment remains intact.
- The animated Hopper was removed from Reviews at every width.
